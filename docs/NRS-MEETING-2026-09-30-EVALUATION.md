# NRS meeting, 30 September — what they asked for against what we actually have

**Internal.** Written the day of the meeting, from the transcript, and checked against the code in both repositories rather than from memory. Every verdict below was verified; where something could not be verified it says so.

**Demo is Friday, 2 October.** Section 7 is what that should look like given the findings.

---

## 1 · The headline

They told us their real problems, which is the most valuable thing that could have come out of that room. There were three, and they named them when asked directly:

1. **Cost centre** — laptops booked to ICT and never reassigned when handed to a person.
2. **Location** — furniture delivered for one building gets diverted to another and the record still shows the original.
3. **Individually unidentifiable assets** — a hundred identical chairs, and no way to say *this* chair is in *that* office.

**On the third, which is their sharpest, we are in better shape than they asked for.** On the first two, we have most of the machinery and a specific missing link in each — and one of those links is a dependency on them, not on us.

Three things need to be faced before Friday:

- **Cost is their number one concern and we have no answer to it.** They raised it four separate times. It is not a software question.
- **Two things we demonstrated verbally on Tuesday do not work the way we said.** Scanning a serial number, and the notification to taggers.
- **No SAP interface in this programme has ever exchanged one message with a real SAP.** Everything SAP-facing they see on Friday is our mock answering itself.

---

## 2 · What they asked for that we have, and can show

### 2.1 · A unique running number per physical unit — their sharpest ask, and we exceed it

Their words: *"there should be a full number that will run from one to infinity… So if I say 150, I should know that."* And the problem behind it: *"I have 100 of these chairs. It's not personalised because it can be moved later… I cannot identify the one that is in your office."*

Cadra gives every physical unit **two** identifiers, both generated by us and neither reusable:

- **`asset_code`** — `NRS-AST-000001`, globally sequential, database-unique, read-only, and impossible for a caller to set. This is literally "chair 150".
- **`asset_tag`** — `HQ-GF-MR-FN-150`, the barcode, built from **building–floor–room–category** plus a running number within that prefix. This is *better* than what they asked for, because the number tells you where the thing is meant to be before you scan it.

And the architecture rule is explicit in the code: one row per physical unit, no quantity field, *"a GRN line of five laptops becomes five assets with five tags. There is no quantity field to send and there never should be."*

**Lead with this on Friday.** It is the answer to the problem they care most about and they have not seen it.

### 2.2 · Serial capture that cannot be bypassed

Exactly as pitched. A goods receipt cannot be submitted until one serial number is captured per accepted unit, per line, and make, model and condition are demanded on the same lines. The refusal reads: *"Row #3 (Laptop): 4 of 5 serial numbers captured. Capture one serial number per accepted unit."*

Better than pitched, and worth saying: the *Accept / Partial / Reject* dropdown is **not trusted on its own**. A receipt marked "Reject" skips capture only if every row is genuinely fully rejected — because otherwise one storekeeper picking the wrong option would take five laptops into stock with no serials and no hand-off.

### 2.3 · Authorisation on transfer and custody — they named it, we built it last week

Their words: *"Today, asset transfer is done without authorization… the asset record should be able to be updated on SAP, where the master record is. That solution, if you can offer something in that area, is good."*

Moving an asset or assigning a custodian raises a request that a **second person** must approve. The asset does not move until they do. Self-approval is refused — *"You raised this request and cannot approve it. The point of an approval is that a second person agrees."* — a second pending request on the same asset is refused, and the audit history now carries **both** people, requester and approver.

This was built on 27 September, two days before they asked for it.

### 2.4 · The asset master does go back to SAP

This is stronger than I expected and it is the direct answer to *"why have we been talking about another system?"*

| Interface | Direction | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **INT-06** asset master create | **Cadra → SAP** | when the tag is affixed |
| **INT-07** SAP asset number returned | SAP → Cadra | response to INT-06 |
| **INT-09** asset transferred | **Cadra → SAP** | on transfer completion |
| **INT-09** custodian changed | **Cadra → SAP** | on custodian change |
| **INT-09** asset disposed | **Cadra → SAP** | on disposal |

Real code, tested, on the same delivery mechanism as everything else, with per-asset visibility of whether SAP has it. **SAP stays the system of record and we push into it** — which is precisely the argument the director was pressing us on, and we can now point at the interfaces rather than assert it.

### 2.5 · The wrong-tag control

Their description of verification: *"the serial number on the laptop matches the tag they're about to affix."*

Affixing refuses a tag number Cadra did not issue for that asset: *"The label on the asset is not the one Cadra issued for it."* There is a second, optional refusal when the scanned serial does not match the asset's serial, and a third when the asset has no serial on file. Tag numbers are never reused, even after scrapping.

### 2.6 · IR number to assets held

*"if you give me your IR number… how many assets are with this person?"* Works — resolve the employee by IR number, then list assets in their custody. Two API calls rather than one; the screen does both.

### 2.7 · Bulk tag printing, and per-unit tag generation

Generate and print in bulk, capped at 500 a time, with an untagged-assets queue to work from. Single and batch print both exist.

### 2.8 · Other things already there

Depreciation (internal to Cadra, never sent — correctly, since SAP owns the financials). Per-asset verification with a genuine expiry, so a verification lapses rather than standing for ever. Rejected quantities, rejected warehouse, condition per line. Disposal with type, method, reason, approver and proceeds.

## 3 · Gaps, worst first

### 3.1 · Cost — no answer, and it is their number one concern

They raised it four times and were explicit about why: *"different vendors have been here. This is always one of the key issues"*, *"the challenge we had there was cost because they will tell you the printer is very expensive"*, and *"we'll know if what you're bringing is more expensive than what we have checked for in the past."*

They also told us the shape of the answer they want:

- They will buy tags with or without us, and they know what tags cost today.
- **They do not need to buy from us.** *"You don't necessarily need to buy them from us… we'll just give you the specifications."* They asked for **specifications and samples.**
- The pitch that landed was efficiency, not cheaper tags. Our own side said it: *"I can take your system and whoever's been printing my tags before continues printing my tags. My system just sends a new code."*

**We owe them, before or at Friday:** a printer specification with a price range, tag material specifications per asset class, and physical samples. None of it exists. This is procurement work, not engineering, and it is the thing most likely to stall the deal.

### 3.2 · Scanning a serial number does not find a tagged asset — and we said it does

Our pitch: *"if you bring 100 laptops inside this room now… I just scan the serial number, it shows me the asset details immediately."* They then singled scanning out as one of the two things they care most about.

**The scan box searches tag number and asset code only. It does not search serial number.** Verified in the lookup controller.

The nuance matters, because part of it does work:

- Scanning the **serial** of an asset that has **not yet been tagged** finds it — the untagged picker does search serials. So the receive → find → tag flow works.
- Scanning the **serial** of an asset that is **already tagged** does not find it. The auditor-with-a-scanner scenario fails.
- Scanning the **tag** always works.

Worse: our own published API documentation says the parameter accepts *"Tag, asset code, or serial number"* on two endpoints. **The documentation is wrong.** That is a false published claim and it should be either fixed in the code or corrected in the document this week — the code fix looks small.

### 3.3 · Nothing notifies the tagging team, in either system

Our pitch: *"A notification will be sent to the relevant… X amount of goods have been received and require tagging."*

Not built anywhere. There is a tagging **queue** — an untagged-assets list, a KPI, filters, a dedicated tag-issuing-unit group — but it is entirely **pull**. Somebody has to go and look. Every notification path in either system is a failure or dead-letter alarm to administrators.

This is a small build and it is the difference between the workflow we described and the workflow that exists. It also bears on their distributed model, where state accountants do the tagging in their own offices and nobody is watching a screen.

### 3.4 · A hundred chairs means typing a hundred serial numbers by hand

**This is the collision between their sharpest requirement and our receiving flow, and it is the most valuable piece of work in this document.**

Cadra will happily give unique running numbers to 100 chairs. But something has to present them as 100 records, and today that something is a storekeeper typing into a textarea. Verified: there is no generation, no sequence, no range expansion, no bulk fill, no paste-expansion — on the server, in the desk form, or in the receiving screen. Serials must be globally unique and duplicates on a line are refused, so they cannot type `CHAIR` a hundred times. They must invent 100 distinct strings.

For laptops this is fine — scan the manufacturer's barcodes. **For furniture, which is exactly the case they raised, it is unusable.**

The fix is the obvious one and it is not large: for a line whose items carry no manufacturer serial, let Vendra mint the unit identifiers itself — a sequence per receipt line, or a prefix plus a range. It needs a decision about what those identifiers look like, which is a question for NRS, not for us.

### 3.5 · Location: Vendra never tells Cadra where anything is

Their second named pain, and the diversion case they described is not solved today.

- Vendra's asset hand-off payload carries **no location, warehouse, building or department** — verified by searching the payload builder and the whole Cadra client. **An asset arrives in the register with no location at all.**
- The receiving screen does not even let the storekeeper choose the accepted warehouse; it comes through from the order.
- Cadra has 1,154 real locations on the demo tenant and a move-with-approval flow, so the destination side is built. The initial placement is missing.

**And there is a consequence worth knowing, because it degrades section 2.1.** The tag number's prefix is derived from building–floor–room. An asset with no location falls back to a brand short code instead. So the location gap quietly spoils the very numbering scheme that answers their sharpest requirement. Fixing the location hand-off improves the thing we most want to show off.

A second, separate limitation: **a move on an asset that SAP has not yet capitalised is logged and dropped, not queued.** Pre-capitalisation moves never reach SAP as moves. Deliberate, with reasoning, but it is a real edge they will eventually hit.

### 3.6 · Cost centre: their number one internal pain, and it is blocked on them

Their case: laptops all booked to ICT, never reassigned when handed out. *"If you don't make the list available to non-current assets, of the people they've given it to."*

- There is **no cost centre on an asset** in Cadra at all. It exists on the employee, fed from SAP.
- The SAP payloads send **cost centre as null, deliberately, in all three places.** The reason is in the code: NRS has not supplied the cost-centre master, and `department` holds 808 distinct free-text values, so deriving one by string matching *"would be a fiction."*
- Vendra echoes SAP's own cost centre back on a goods receipt unchanged, and never reassigns it.

**So we cannot solve their cost-centre problem until they give us the cost-centre master.** That is worth saying on Friday, plainly and without defensiveness: it turns our largest functional gap into a concrete request that puts the next move on their side. Ask for the cost-centre list and the asset-class mapping (the second is also pending and blocks the SAP asset class on every create).

### 3.7 · Reprinting a faded tag — built underneath, no button

Their auditor scenario: *"you notice an air conditioner tag is weak or it's no longer very clear. You can reprint a tag for that particular asset."*

The model does it properly — request replacement, approve, scrap the old label, mint a new number, keep the chain from old to new so history survives. **But there is no API route and no control in the React app.** It is reachable only from the Odoo back office. Also, the replacement reason is never written by the action, so "damaged" has to be typed on the form.

### 3.8 · Verification by scanning is not a flow

*"then you talk of asset verification. My vision is tied to the tag, the scanning."*

Per-asset verification is genuinely good — it records who and when, it expires on a per-category interval, and a monthly job makes lapsed verifications lapse. What does not exist: any campaign or session, any walk-round worklist, any way to verify by scanning. The camera scanner feeds lookup only; it is not wired to verify. Verification is a button on one asset's page at a time.

For a national register with state offices, that is the gap between a feature and an exercise.

### 3.9 · IR number format is not validated — and they raised exactly this

*"Sometimes the IR number is not captured properly, where it's supposed to be zero. Sometimes we see O."*

No format validation at all. `ir_number` is plain text with a uniqueness constraint, so `IR0012` and `IRO012` are two different employees, both can exist, and uniqueness will never catch it. Lookups are exact match, so neither finds the other.

This is an hour's work and it answers a complaint they volunteered. Cheap credibility.

### 3.10 · "Printed" does not mean printed

The React barcode station prints through the browser's print dialog. The label state only advances to `printed` when a server-side PDF is rendered. **So every print made from the station leaves the label at `issued`.** Do not quote `printed` as a count of labels printed, and do not build a report on it.

## 4 · Where our own pitch went further than the product

Not to relitigate the meeting — to avoid repeating it on Friday in front of more people.

| We said | The truth |
|---|---|
| *"in a matter of 3 or 4 seconds, the asset code is generated"* | The hand-off is queued at submit and picked up by a worker — normally a few seconds when the workers are healthy, with a five-minute retry sweep behind it. **No one has ever measured it.** And there is a documented three-day period this month when nothing reached Cadra or SAP at all and nothing said so. Say *"normally a few seconds, with a retry behind it"* and stop there. |
| *"a notification will be sent… requiring tagging"* | Not built. §3.3. |
| *"I just scan the serial number, it shows me the asset details"* | Only for untagged assets. §3.2. |
| *"the maintenance log… vendor logs maintenance notes… every 3 months people get notified"* | Built — but **they told us they do not want it.** See §5.3. |
| *"SAP automatically knows that this laptop, this Dell with this serial number, has this asset code"* | The interfaces exist (§2.4) and have **never run against a real SAP**. See §5.2. |

## 5 · The three things that decide whether this goes ahead

### 5.1 · "Challenge SAP" — the objection to answer properly on Friday

The director came back to it repeatedly and his version was specific: depreciation, acquisition and disposal already flow straight into the financials, and *"I will not want to be bringing this thing manually into the system again."*

The middleware answer given in the room was the right one, and §2.4 means we can now demonstrate it rather than argue it: **SAP remains the master, nothing becomes manual, and every asset change we capture is pushed into SAP.** Show INT-06 and INT-09 doing that. His objection is not really about architecture — it is *"do not make my financial reporting worse."* The honest answer is that we do not touch it.

### 5.2 · No SAP interface has ever run against SAP

Stated plainly, because it will decide how Friday is framed. Verified in the repositories:

- **INT-01** — every inbound purchase order on the deployed site was sent by a person with Postman or Swagger, using a body copied from our own published example, with placeholders still in it. Fourteen requests between 2 and 16 September, none retried, no duplicate ever answered.
- **INT-02** — the mock is all there has ever been.
- Our own go-live document says it: *"Every SAP interface in this repository has only ever been proved against our own mock."*

There is also a correction outstanding. **NRS was told at some point that INT-01 could be considered closed, and our own report said that should be corrected.** I do not know whether it was. If it was not, Friday is the wrong place for them to find out — better to volunteer it beforehand as *"our endpoint is proved, SAP's side has not sent us anything yet, and here is what we need from them."*

### 5.3 · Maintenance — they do not want it, so stop selling it

*"Then another one is maintenance, which is completely outside our own. We are not even interested… Not our duty."* Facilities' job, not theirs.

We have a preventive-maintenance module with scheduling and crons, and we pitched it at length. **Take it out of Friday.** Showing it again spends a slot on something they declined and signals we were not listening. Keep it in the product for whoever does own it later.

### 5.4 · The commercial risk nobody said out loud

*"we have told them some of our concerns about SAP… I think they are working on an upgrade of SAP. And we've told them about this location and the rest, which they are bringing in to SAP. So it's like, as if we're already solving the problem. So by the time we fix the location issue, and IT gives us the people they have already assigned their assets to, it's as good as fine."*

**If the SAP upgrade delivers location and IR numbers on assets, two of their three named pains disappear without us.** Mr. Richard was in the room and appears to be on that side.

What the upgrade almost certainly does **not** give them: a gate that stops a goods receipt without serial numbers, a unique identity for the hundredth identical chair, tagging and scanning, a second person approving a transfer, and downstream systems reaching assets without touching SAP. Those five are the case. **Find out what is in that upgrade before Friday** — ideally by asking Mr. Richard directly.

## 6 · What to ask them for

Each of these converts something we cannot finish into something waiting on them:

1. **The cost-centre master** — unblocks §3.6, their own first-named pain.
2. **The asset-class mapping** for SAP — currently blocks the asset class on every master create.
3. **What is in the SAP upgrade**, specifically on location and IR numbers (§5.4).
4. **What a unit identifier should look like for assets with no manufacturer serial** (§3.4) — their numbering, not ours to invent.
5. **A test SAP environment.** Still the largest single hole in the programme, and it has been outstanding for weeks.
6. **The distribution list from ICT** — they raised it themselves as the thing that would let them fix cost centres.

## 7 · Recommendation for Friday

**Show, in this order:**

1. A purchase order arriving from SAP, and the storekeeper pulling it up by PO number.
2. Receiving against it — and **deliberately try to post without all the serials**, so they see the refusal. That gate is the product.
3. Warranty and condition captured at the same moment.
4. The units appearing in the register with **their own running numbers** — §2.1, and dwell on it, because it is the answer to the chair problem.
5. Tag issued, and **an attempt to affix the wrong tag being refused.**
6. Bulk tag generation and printing from the station.
7. A transfer raised by one person and **approved by a second**, with both names in the history.
8. IR number in, assets held out.
9. The audit trail on one asset, end to end.

**Do not show:** maintenance (§5.3), scanning a serial on an already-tagged asset (§3.2), or anything that depends on a notification arriving (§3.3).

**Say out loud, unprompted:** that SAP's side has not yet sent us live traffic (§5.2), that cost centre needs their master before we can help (§3.6), and that the tag and printer costs are coming as specifications and samples rather than a sales quote (§3.1).

**Before Friday, if there is engineering time, in this order of value per hour:** IR number validation (§3.9, ~1 hour), serial lookup in the scan box (§3.2, small, and it fixes a false published claim), and the location on the Cadra hand-off (§3.5, because it also fixes the tag prefix).
